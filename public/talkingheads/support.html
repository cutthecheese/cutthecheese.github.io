Talking Heads — Support

Talking Heads puts little animated heads in the bottom corners of your screen. Whatever your Mac plays — a video, a call, a podcast — appears as live speech bubbles, one head per voice, transcribed on your Mac as it is spoken.

Getting started

  1. Open Talking Heads. It lives in the menu bar (the speech bubbles icon).
  2. Allow System Audio Recording when macOS asks. If you missed it: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording.
  3. The first time, the speech model downloads (about 700 MB); a ring over the head shows the progress.
  4. Play something with people talking.

In the menu

  • Left · what your Mac plays and Right · your microphone: turn each on or off and pick its language (or let it detect the language).
  • Recognition: Parakeet (default, fast, 25 European languages), Whisper (99 languages) or Apple Speech.
  • Translate Bubbles: show every bubble in a language of your choice.
  • Show: heads and bubbles, only heads, or only bubbles.
  • Show Emotions (Beta): faces react to what is said.
  • Music Listening After: how long sound without words lasts before a head starts listening to the music.

Common questions

The heads don't appear.
They only come up while something is playing, and slide away after a few seconds of quiet. Check that Talking Heads is allowed under Screen & System Audio Recording in System Settings.
Two people share one head, or one person has two.
Voices are told apart by how they sound. Words show at once and move to the right head within a second or two. Very similar voices, or people talking over each other, can still be mixed up.
The words are in the wrong language.
Pick the language under the channel in the menu instead of detecting it.
The microphone head shows what my Mac plays.
Without headphones the microphone hears your speakers too. Use headphones, or turn the microphone off.
